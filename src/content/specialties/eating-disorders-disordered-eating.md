**_Disordered eating_** is a term used when someone doesn't meet diagnostic criteria for an **_eating disorder_**. This could look like your health or behaviours not being seen as severe enough for an eating disorder diagnosis. However, this does not mean that your relationship to food and body isn't causing you a significant amount of distress. **_Your pain and struggle is real_**, whether you have a diagnosis or not. 

**____________________________________________________________________________________**



**If you're living with an eating disorder or disordered eating, you may:**

• **_Over-eat_** when you're feeling anxious, bored, sad, angry, etc.

• **_Hide_** or **_eat_** food in secret

• Spend minutes, maybe hours, **_checking your body in the mirror_**

• **_Limit the amount of food you eat in a day_**, and you may bypass or no longer have hunger cues

• Exercise, throw up, or use other strategies to **_compensate for what you ate_**

• **_Check your weight_** multiple times per week, maybe even daily, and any fluctuations impact your mood and self-confidence

• Have a **_very strict diet and exercise plan_**, eating only "healthy" foods

• **_Avoid environments_** where there's food or your body may be seen (e.g., not going to restaurants or avoiding pools)

• Go through **_phases_** where your relationship to food and body feels stable, but then a few months later it feels like **_it's taken over your life again_**

• Keep your challenges a **_secret from everyone you know_**, leaving you feeling a deep sense of loneliness and shame



**____________________________________________________________________________________**

**_You don't need a diagnosis to seek support or to feel like your relationship to food and body is "bad enough" to get help._**


Through my training and work, I began to understand that eating disorders and disordered eating may be **_tools_** people used to **_cope with overwhelming experiences_** in their lives. I have also found that eating disorders don't typically exist in a vacuum, meaning other factors in our lives impact our relationship to food and our bodies and vice versa. Trauma, interpersonal relationship challenges, and life transitions are common experiences that can often maintain and intensify challenges with food and body image.

This is the "why" behind how I approach counselling for eating disorders and disordered eating. I intentionally create space to explore your relationship to food and your body, while also looking at other areas of your life that may be impacting your recovery. We will work to understand what the eating disorder/disordered eating may be helping you cope with, while developing new skills to help you turn towards it less, building a life that feels more fulfilling and aligned with who you are.
