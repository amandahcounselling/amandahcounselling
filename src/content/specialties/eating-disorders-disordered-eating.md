**_Disordered eating_** is a term used when someone doesn't meet diagnostic criteria for an **_eating disorder_**. This could look like your health or behaviours not being seen as severe enough for a diagnosis. But, this doesn't mean that your relationship to food and body isn't causing you a significant amount of distress. **_Your pain and struggle is real_**, whether you have a diagnosis or not. 

**__________________________________________________________________________**


**If you're living with an eating disorder or disordered eating, you may:**

• **_Over-eat_** when you're feeling anxious, bored, sad, angry, etc.

• **_Hide_** or eat food in **_secret_**

• Spend minutes, maybe hours, **_checking your body in the mirror_**

• **_Limit the amount of food you eat in a day_**, and you may bypass or no longer have hunger cues

• Exercise, throw up, or use other strategies to **_compensate for what you ate_**

• **_Check your weight_** multiple times per week, maybe even daily, and any fluctuations impact your mood and self-confidence

• Have a **_very strict diet and exercise plan_**, eating only "healthy" foods

• **_Avoid environments_** where there's food or your body may be seen (e.g., not going to restaurants or avoiding pools)

• Go through **_phases_** where your relationship to food and body feels stable, but then a few months later it feels like **_it's taken over your life again_**

• Keep your challenges a **_secret from everyone you know_**, leaving you feeling a deep sense of loneliness and shame


**__________________________________________________________________________**


**_You don't need a diagnosis to seek support or to feel like your relationship to food and body is "bad enough" to get help._**


Through my training and work, I began to understand that eating disorders and disordered eating may be **_tools_** people used to **_cope with overwhelming experiences_** in their lives; while often times our perception of our body can be a significant part of eating disorders and disordered eating, they aren't the only aspect of them. That stressful day at work, difficult conversation with a loved one, moment that startled you, or promotion you wanted can all create feelings that you may not have the capacity to hold right now, so you may turn to eating disorder or disordered eating behaviours to try and comfort yourself in those moments.


This is the "why" behind my approach to counselling for eating disorders and disordered eating. I intentionally create space to explore your relationship to food and your body, while also looking at other areas of your life that may be impacting your recovery, including interpersonal relationship challenges, life transitions, anxiety, depression, and trauma. We will work to understand what the eating disorder/disordered eating may be helping you cope with, while developing new skills to help you turn towards it less, building a life that feels more fulfilling and aligned with who you are.
