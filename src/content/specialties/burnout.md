Do any of these sound familiar?

You chronically over-commit to things, like taking on extra shifts at work to make sure you're coming across as a team player or saying "yes" to all of the social plans (fear of missing out/FOMO is so real)
Even though you know you need to slow down, when you do get a moment to rest it feels unfamiliar (and maybe uncomfortable), so you fill your schedule to the brim
You're the friend or family member who supports everyone else at the drop of a hat, but you never as for the same in return
You have high expectations for yourself but have too many tasks to complete in a day that exceed your actual capacity, but this feels hard to confront or accept
You've been juggling raising kids, taking care of aging parents, trying to advance your career, or working to make enough money to survive in this economy

Whatever your reason may be, burnout is a real experience that effects so many people, but it is different from depression. Burnout tends to be in response to an immediate and/or long-standing stressor in a specific area of our lives. We may still find joy in our hobbies and in other aspects of life, and once the stressor is removed or dealt with, the feelings of numbness and overwhelm tend to improve.

While burnout can be an unpleasant experience, it can also serve as a signal that something isn't working for us. The numbness, exhaustion, overwhelm, and brain fog can feel like they're making our lives unbearable, but if we can turn towards them and explore what may be causing them, we may learn a great deal about ourselves and what we need to move forward. Our work together can explore the "why" behind the burnout. For some, the "why" may be a career that doesn't feel aligned, high-expectations of self, lack of social support, hyper-independence, or a challenging economy.
